# NASA Human Research Program Evidence Report: Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS)

## 1. Executive Metadata & Report Architecture

This document provides a comprehensive extraction of the epidemiological, clinical, biomechanical, and operational evidence documented in the NASA Human Research Program (HRP) Evidence Report, *Risk of Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS)*, approved for public release on April 07, 2022.

*   **Primary HRP Report Authors**: Steven S. Laurie, Brandon R. Macias, Laura P. Pardon, Tyson Brunstetter, William J. Tarver, C. Robert Gibson, Scott H. Greenwald, Karina Marshall-Goebel, Jessica V. Jasien, Sara Mason, Ann Tsung, David J. Alexander, Douglas R. Hamilton, Stuart M. C. Lee, Thomas H. Mader, Christian Otto, Cherie M. Oubre, Anastas F. Pass, Steven H. Platts, Jessica M. Scott, Scott M. Smith, Michael B. Stenger, Christian M. Westby, Susana B. Zanello, and Sara R. Zwart.
*   **Operational Definition of SANS**: A distinct neuro-ophthalmic syndrome resulting from prolonged microgravity exposure during long-duration spaceflight (LDSF), characterized by optic disc edema (ODE), posterior globe flattening (GF), chorioretinal folds, and hyperopic refractive error shifts.
*   **HRP Risk Status**: Active research risk managed by the Human Health Countermeasures Element at the NASA Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center.

## 2. Clinical Epidemiology & Quantitative Diagnostic Baselines

### 2.1 Prevalence Across Astronaut & Cosmonaut Cohorts
Occupational health surveillance by the NASA Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) establishes that approximately **70% of crewmembers** participating in long-duration International Space Station (ISS) missions present with one or more objective ocular signs of SANS.

| Clinical Finding | Objective Diagnostic Metric | Population Prevalence (% of USOS Long-Duration Crew) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Any SANS Finding** | Presence of ODE, GF, folds, or refractive shift | **70%** |
| **Subclinical Optic Disc Edema (ODE)** | Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) $\Delta\text{TRT} \ge 19.4\ \mu\text{m}$ | **67% – 70%** |
| **Clinically Significant ODE** | Modified Frisén Scale Grade $\ge 1$ via Fundoscopy | **15%** |
| **Chorioretinal Folds** | OCT / Fundoscopic undulations of Bruch's Membrane / RPE | **20%** |
| **Globe Flattening (GF)** | MRI posterior scleral volume displacement / Biometry | **20%** |
| **Hyperopic Refractive Shift** | Cycloplegic Spherical Equivalent Shift $\ge +0.75\text{ D}$ | **15%** |

*   **Historical Cosmonaut Cohort Data**: Early ophthalmoscopic evaluations aboard the Mir space station (Mayasnikov and Stepanova, 2008) identified 8 cases of ODE among 16 cosmonauts (a clinical prevalence rate of ~19%), ranging from partial to moderate optic disc swelling.
*   **Sentinel Astronaut Series**: The sentinel case series by Mader et al. (2011) evaluated 7 male ISS astronauts with post-flight visual acuity changes. Five presented with ODE (2 unilateral Frisén Grade 1, 1 bilateral Frisén Grade 1, 1 asymmetric bilateral Frisén Grade 1, and 1 asymmetric bilateral ODE with Grade 3 in the right eye and Grade 1 in the left eye). A 9-year follow-up study on a repeat LDSF flyer documented recurrence of unilateral Frisén Grade 1 ODE five months into the second long-duration mission.

### 2.2 On-Orbit Ophthalmic Hardware Progression
NASA's Flight Medicine Clinic has evolved inflight retinal diagnostic hardware to track SANS progression:
1.  **2009**: Modified PanOptic ophthalmoscope (Welch Allyn) attached to a QuickCam web camera (Logitech).
2.  **2013**: Merge Fundus Camera (Merge Healthcare/OIS) providing higher resolution posterior pole imaging.
3.  **2013–2017**: SPECTRALIS SD-OCT 1 (Heidelberg Engineering), upgraded to SPECTRALIS OCT 2 in 2017 for inflight radial ONH scans.
4.  **2019**: MultiColor Imaging (MCI) technology utilizing 3 simultaneous scanning lasers (green, blue, infrared) to generate high-contrast false-color posterior pole photographs.
5.  **2021**: Pictor Plus portable fundus camera (Volk Optical) deployed to replace the Merge device.

### 2.3 Optic Disc Edema (ODE) Quantification & OCT Thresholds
Direct ophthalmoscopy relies on the subjective, categorical Frisén scale (where Grade 1 represents edema extending $270^\circ$ to $359^\circ$ around the disc margin in a C-shaped pattern starting nasally). NASA deployed Spectral-Domain Optical Coherence Tomography (SD-OCT) to establish objective, continuous quantitative metrics.

*   **BMO-250 $\mu\text{m}$ Total Retinal Thickness (TRT) Diagnostic Threshold**: TRT measured in an annular region extending from Bruch's Membrane Opening (BMO) to $250\ \mu\text{m}$ retinal eccentricity serves as the primary quantitative biomarker for SANS ODE. Based on repeat 4-month body posture reliability studies in non-flown astronauts, a baseline change ($\Delta\text{TRT}$) exceeding **$19.4\ \mu\text{m}$** establishes the 95% confidence interval upper limit for normal measurement and physiological variability, defining the threshold for early subclinical ODE.
*   **In-Flight Neuroretinal Rim & Retinal Dynamics**: Prospective longitudinal data from the NASA Ocular Health Study (Macias et al., 2020) tracked ocular structural changes across 6-month and 1-year ISS missions:
    *   **Minimum Rim Width (MRW)**: Tissue within the optic nerve head (ONH) increased by an average of **$12.5\ \mu\text{m}$ on Flight Day 10 (FD10)** and escalated to **$35.7\ \mu\text{m}$ on FD150**.
    *   **BMO-250 $\mu\text{m}$ TRT**: Increased by an average of **$11.9\ \mu\text{m}$ on FD10** and **$27.6\ \mu\text{m}$ on FD150**.
    *   **BMO 250–500 $\mu\text{m}$ TRT**: Increased by **$4.7\ \mu\text{m}$ on FD10** and **$12.9\ \mu\text{m}$ on FD150**.
*   **Resolution & Recovery Profiles**: MRW and TRT measures return to baseline approximately **90 days post-return to Earth** in standard 6-month flyers. However, 1-year mission crewmembers demonstrate a partial rapid recovery during the first 1–3 days post-landing, followed by prolonged recovery extending beyond **365 days post-flight**.
*   **Laterality & Sex Distribution**: Objective OCT data confirm that ODE develops in both male and female crewmembers with equal frequency, with no statistically significant difference between right and left eyes (mean inter-eye difference of $8 \pm 13\ \mu\text{m}$ at FD120, $P = 0.0662$).

## 3. Ocular Morphological & Refractive Alterations

### 3.1 Chorioretinal Folds
Chorioretinal folds develop in ~20% of long-duration crewmembers.
*   **Classification**: Folds are categorized as either **Choroidal Folds** (undulations of Bruch’s membrane and the retinal pigment epithelium) or **Retinal Folds** (undulations of the inner retinal surface outside the immediate peripapillary disc).
*   **Progression & Permanence**: Submacular choroidal folds progressively worsen with spaceflight duration (observed during 1-year ISS missions). Unlike ODE, chorioretinal folds can persist for years post-landing (documented up to 7+ years post-flight by Mader et al., 2017) and may represent permanent mechanical structural remodeling of the posterior sclera.

### 3.2 Globe Flattening (GF) & Axial Length Shortening
Posterior scleral flattening occurs due to anterior mechanical displacement of the posterior eye wall surrounding the ONH.
*   **MRI Volumetric Quantification**: Prospective 3D MRI mapping (Sater et al., 2021) in 10 long-duration astronauts demonstrated a mean post-flight vitreous volume displacement at the ONH of **$9.88 \pm 5.31\text{ mm}^3$ on Return Day 1 (R+1)** ($P < 0.001$), which partially persisted at **$7.21 \pm 5.39\text{ mm}^3$ at R+360** ($P < 0.01$).
*   **Anterior ONH Displacement**: Scleral flattening causes the ONH to displace anteriorly toward the vitreous chamber by a mean of **$200 \pm 110\ \mu\text{m}$ at R+1** and **$140 \pm 100\ \mu\text{m}$ at R+360**.
*   **Macular Axial Length Shortening**: Optical biometry (Zeiss IOLMaster 700) demonstrates that axial shortening at the macula is distinct from ONH flattening. Foveal axial length shortening averages **$120 \pm 70\ \mu\text{m}$ at R+1** ($0.08\text{ mm}$ mean reduction in the Ocular Health cohort; maximum documented reduction of $0.53\text{ mm}$ in individual case studies).

### 3.3 Refractive Error Shifts & Anterior Segment Dynamics
*   **Hyperopic Shift Magnitude**: Approximately 15% of long-duration astronauts exhibit an excessive hyperopic shift defined as $\ge +0.75\text{ D}$ (cycloplegic spherical equivalent). Individual shifts up to **$+2.00\text{ D}$** have been documented during single 6-month missions. In 29% of short-duration Space Shuttle flyers and 60% of long-duration ISS flyers, crewmembers report subjective degradation of near visual acuity requiring corrective lenses.
*   **Optical Component Mechanics**: A $1.00\text{ D}$ hyperopic refractive shift terrestrially corresponds to an axial shortening of $\sim 0.35\text{ mm}$. In spaceflight, macular axial shortening ($0.08\text{ mm}$) accounts for a $+0.13\text{ D}$ hyperopic shift. Anterior chamber depth decreases by an average of **$0.09\text{ mm}$**, which induces a mild counteracting myopic shift due to increased corneal-lens optical power convergence.

### 3.4 Choroidal Expansion & Vascular Engorgement
*   **In-Flight Choroidal Thickening**: Longitudinal OCT imaging (Greenwald et al., 2021) reveals a mean subfoveal choroidal expansion of **$\sim 30\ \mu\text{m}$ at 1 month** in microgravity, reaching a mean of **$\sim 40\ \mu\text{m}$ by Month 5**, with extreme individual increases reaching up to **$174\ \mu\text{m}$**.
*   **Outflow Mechanics**: Choroidal engorgement is caused by cerebral venous congestion impeding vortex vein outflow. Acute 15-minute exposure to $25\text{ mmHg}$ Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP) during spaceflight does not reduce choroidal thickness, indicating that vascular distension and capillary filtration changes require prolonged fluid unloading to reverse.

### 3.5 Secondary Ocular Pathology Findings
*   **Cotton Wool Spots (CWS)**: 8 total cases of CWS (microvascular retinal ischemia) have been identified (6 in the left eye, 2 in the right eye). Most diagnoses occurred post-flight (ranging from R+19 days to R+9 years). Space radiation exposure and capillary ischemia are hypothesized etiologies.
*   **Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachments (PEDs) & Cysts**: High-density OCT protocols revealed intraretinal and ONH cysts that resolve within 2 months post-flight. Retinal PEDs (separation of RPE basement membrane from Bruch's membrane) expand vertically over areas of pachychoroid during flight and resolve post-flight without visual acuity loss.
*   **Retinal Microvasculature Density**: Vessel Generation Analysis (VESGEN) demonstrated significant post-flight decreases in retinal microvascular length density in small capillary beds ($11/16$ eyes across 8 crewmembers), directly correlating with the magnitude of TRT swelling.

## 4. Intracranial Dynamics, Brain Structure, and Hydrostatic Mechanisms

### 4.1 Spaceflight-Induced Brain Structural Plasticity
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) studies comparing pre-flight to post-flight brain morphology demonstrate significant intracranial structural remodeling:

*   **Ventricular Volume Expansion**: Ventricular system volume increases by **10% to 16% (an absolute expansion of $1\text{ to }3\text{ mL}$)** following long-duration missions (Roberts et al., 2017; Kramer et al., 2020). Ventricular enlargement persists above baseline at 365 days post-landing.
*   **Cranial Displacement & CSF Space Narrowing**: Microgravity causes an upward volumetric displacement of the brain within the calvarium, resulting in significant narrowing of the central sulcus, supervermian cistern, and calcarine sulcus, alongside deformation of the optic chiasm and pituitary gland (in 6 of 11 crewmembers).
*   **Gray/White Matter Redistribution**: Gray matter volume decreases in orbitofrontal and temporal cortexes and cerebellar Crus II, accompanied by post-flight expansion of CSF volume in ventral brain regions and persistent elevation of CSF stroke volume through the cerebral aqueduct.

### 4.2 Intracranial Pressure (ICP) & Hydrostatic Fluid Redistribution
*   **Cephalad Fluid Shift Volume**: Loss of the $1\text{g}$ head-to-foot hydrostatic pressure gradient (where standing arterial pressure ranges from $200\text{ mmHg}$ at the foot to $70\text{ mmHg}$ at the head) causes an immediate cephalad fluid redistribution of **1 to 2 Liters** of blood and interstitial fluid.
*   **Terrestrial & Parabolic Flight ICP Normative Values**:
    *   *Mayo Clinic Normative Population* ($n=33,922$; Fleischman et al., 2012): Mean supine ICP in adults aged 20–49 is **$15.6 \pm 3.7\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$** ($11.5 \pm 2.7\text{ mmHg}$).
    *   *Healthy Volunteers* ($n=11$; Eklund et al., 2016): Mean supine ICP is **$14.3 \pm 2.0\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$** ($10.5 \pm 1.5\text{ mmHg}$).
    *   *Invasive Parabolic Flight Ommaya Reservoir Dynamics* (Lawley et al., 2017): Terrestrial upright posture yields $4 \pm 1\text{ mmHg}$ ($5.4\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$). Terrestrial supine posture yields $15 \pm 2\text{ mmHg}$ ($20.4\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$). In microgravity ($0\text{g}$ parabolic phase), ICP falls from supine to **$14\text{ to }16\text{ mmHg}$ ($19\text{ to }21.8\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$)**. This proves microgravity does not produce pathological intracranial hypertension ($>25\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$), but rather eliminates the daily postural ICP reduction provided by standing.
*   **Post-Flight Lumbar Puncture Opening Pressures**: In $<10$ crewmembers undergoing clinical post-flight lumbar puncture for Frisén Grade $\ge 1$ ODE, opening pressures ranged from **$18\text{ to }28.5\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$** ($13.2\text{ to }21.0\text{ mmHg}$).

### 4.3 Venous Congestion, Lymphatic, & Glymphatic Pathways
*   **Internal Jugular Vein (IJV) Hemodynamics**: Ultrasonic imaging aboard the ISS (Marshall-Goebel et al., 2019) documented significant IJV cross-sectional area expansion, elevated venous pressure, blood flow stasis, and retrograde (reverse) venous flow in long-duration crewmembers.
*   **Cervical Lymphatic CSF Drainage**: Extracranial cervical lymphatics situated in the nasal submucosa at the cribriform plate clear up to **50% of CSF volume**. Microgravity-induced fluid congestion impairs low-pressure ($0\text{ to }20\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$) lymphatic drainage pathways.
*   **Ocular Glymphatic System**: CSF transport through astrocyte Aquaporin-4 (AQP4) water channels into paravascular spaces clearing $\beta$-amyloid and metabolic waste is modulated by translaminar pressure differences ($\text{IOP} - \text{ICP}$). In microgravity, loss of normal translaminar pressure gradients drives glymphatic stasis, interstitial fluid accumulation, and neuroretinal edema at the optic nerve head.

## 5. Comparative Pathophysiological Matrix: Terrestrial IIH vs. Spaceflight SANS

The hallmark clinical sign of SANS—optic disc edema—resembles terrestrial Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH / Pseudotumor Cerebri). However, critical demographic, anatomic, and physiological distinctions confirm that SANS is an independent clinical entity.

| Clinical / Diagnostic Axis | Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension (IIH) | Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demographics & Sex** | $\ge 90\%$ Female; childbearing age | Equal incidence in Males and Females; mean age late 40s |
| **Body Mass Index (BMI)** | 100% Overweight; 88% Obese (Wall et al., 2014) | Normal astronaut BMI range |
| **Subjective Symptoms** | Severe headache (84%), transient vision obscurations (69%), pulsatile tinnitus (52%), photophobia (48%), diplopia (18%) | Near vision complaints (48% of long-duration crew due to hyperopic shift); **no IIH-type headaches** |
| **Optic Disc Edema (Frisén)** | Frisén Grades 0–5; **Grade 2 most common** | Frisén Grades 0–3; **Grades 0–1 most common** (90% Grade 1) |
| **ONH Anatomic Deformation** | Gross anterior displacement; peripapillary Bruch's Membrane Opening (BMO) bows **anteriorly toward vitreous** | General ONH expansion; BMO bows **posteriorly away from vitreous** (mean $9.9\ \mu\text{m}$ posterior shift) |
| **Visual Field Defects** | Universal; enlarged blind spot with arcuate scotomas (perimetric mean deviation $-2\text{ to }-7\text{ dB}$) | Extremely rare; 2 post-flight enlarged blind spots, 1 transient in-flight scotoma (resolved) |
| **Ocular Side Bias** | $>95\%$ Bilateral presentation | Bilateral involvement confirmed by quantitative OCT ($\Delta\text{TRT}$ difference between eyes $8 \pm 13\ \mu\text{m}$) |
| **Intracranial Pressure (ICP)** | Pathologically elevated: Mean **$34.4 \pm 8.7\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$** (Range: $21.0\text{ to }67.0\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$) | Modestly elevated post-flight: **$18.0\text{ to }28.5\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$** ($n < 10$ with ODE) |
| **MRI Cerebral Ventricles** | Ventricles normal size or **slit-like / compressed** | **Ventricular volume enlargement (10% to 16% / 1–3 mL)**; upward brain displacement within calvarium |
| **Systemic Vascular Signs** | Associated with peripheral edema and orthostatic sodium retention | Associated with IJV engorgement, flow stasis, and retrograde venous blood flow |

## 6. Individual Susceptibility Factors & Genetic Risk Profiling

### 6.1 1-Carbon Metabolic Pathway Polymorphisms
Biochemical analysis of astronauts (Zwart et al., 2012, 2016, 2019) reveals that genetic variations in the folate- and B12-dependent 1-carbon metabolic pathway strongly predict SANS susceptibility and retinal thickening.

*   **Circulating Metabolite Elevations**: Astronauts who develop SANS exhibit **25% to 45% higher pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight serum concentrations** of:
    *   Homocysteine (Hcy)
    *   Cystathionine
    *   2-Methylcitric acid
    *   Methylmalonic acid
*   **Predictive Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs)**:
    *   **$MTRR\text{ A66G}$ (Methionine Synthase Reductase)**: Homozygous or heterozygous recessive G allele is significantly associated with an increased incidence of choroidal folds and Cotton Wool Spots.
    *   **$SHMT1\text{ C1420T}$ (Serine Hydroxymethyltransferase)**: The C allele strongly predicts post-flight Optic Disc Edema ($\Delta\text{TRT}$ elevation).
*   **Genotype-Bed Rest Interaction**: Test subjects carrying **3 or 4 risk alleles** across 1-carbon metabolic genes demonstrated significantly greater retinal nerve fiber layer and total retinal thickening during 30-day strict head-down tilt bed rest compared to subjects carrying **0 to 2 risk alleles**.

### 6.2 Biomechanical & Structural Risk Factors
*   **Optic Cup Configuration ("Disc at Risk")**: Crewmembers with small, crowded optic cups (small cup-to-disc ratio) possess higher scleral canal packing density, predisposing them to nerve fiber compression and ODE during cephalad fluid shifts.
*   **Body Mass Correlation**: Higher pre-flight body mass correlates with increased incidence of ODE and choroidal folds (Buckey et al., 2018).

## 7. Terrestrial Analog Evidence: Strict Head-Down Tilt Bed Rest (HDTBR)

Ground-based analogs utilize $6^\circ$ Head-Down Tilt Bed Rest ($6^\circ\text{ HDTBR}$) to simulate the headward hydrostatic fluid shift.

### 7.1 "Strict" HDTBR Protocol Realization
*   **Standard vs. Strict HDTBR Paradigm**: Historical 70-day bed rest studies (Taibbi et al., 2016) failed to produce ODE because subjects used standard pillows and propped their torsos on forearms for meals. Lawley et al. (2017) demonstrated that pillow usage reduces ICP by **$4\text{ mmHg}$**, neutralizing SANS pathogenesis.
*   **Strict HDTBR Rules**: Precludes traditional pillows (only contoured neck supports maintaining $6^\circ$ tilt are permitted) and forbids any elbow propping.

### 7.2 Quantitative Ocular Findings in Strict HDTBR
*   **ODE Induction Rate**: Strict $6^\circ\text{ HDTBR}$ induces ODE in **45% of subjects by Frisén grading** (Grades 1–2) and **73% to 75% of subjects by OCT TRT criteria** ($\Delta\text{TRT} > 19.4\ \mu\text{m}$).
*   **TRT Expansion Kinetics**:
    *   HDTBR Day 15: Mean TRT increase of **$+21\ \mu\text{m}\text{ to }+36\ \mu\text{m}$.**
    *   HDTBR Day 30–58: Mean TRT increase escalates to **$+37\ \mu\text{m}\text{ to }+54\ \mu\text{m}$.**
*   **Normocapnic vs. Hypercapnic Verification**: Initial strict HDTBR included $0.5\%\text{ CO}_2$ ($4\text{ mmHg}\ p\text{CO}_2$). Subsequent 60-day normocapnic strict HDTBR (AGBRESA study; Laurie et al., 2021) produced identical ODE rates ($75\%$ TRT elevation) and chorioretinal fold formation ($25\%$ incidence), proving that elevated ambient $\text{CO}_2$ is **not a causal factor** for SANS.

### 7.3 Environmental $\text{CO}_2$ & Partial Gravity Thresholds
*   **Cabin $\text{CO}_2$ Micro-environment**: ISS operational limit is managed to a 24-hour average of $\le 3\text{ mmHg}$. Computational fluid dynamics modeling (Son et al., 2002) demonstrates that without facial ventilation fans, local $p\text{CO}_2$ around a sleeping crewmember's mouth can rise above **$9\text{ mmHg}$ within 10 minutes**.
*   **Partial Gravity Venous Unloading Threshold**: Parabolic flight parabolic exposures (Lee et al., 2020b) demonstrate that cerebral venous congestion is not reduced until the head-to-foot gravitational vector exceeds **$0.50\text{g}$**.

### 7.4 Artificial Gravity (cAG vs. iAG) Bed Rest Outcomes
The 60-day AGBRESA study evaluated short-arm centrifugation generating $0.3\text{g}$ at the eye for 30 minutes daily:
*   **Control (No AG)**: Mean TRT increase of **$+35.9\ \mu\text{m}$** at Day 58.
*   **Continuous AG (cAG - 30 min continuous)**: Mean TRT increase of **$+36.5\ \mu\text{m}$.**
*   **Intermittent AG (iAG - $6 \times 5\text{ min}$ with 3 min breaks)**: Mean TRT increase of **$+27.6\ \mu\text{m}$.**
*   *Conclusion*: 30 minutes of daily artificial gravity at $0.3\text{g}$ fails to prevent ODE or chorioretinal fold development during strict bed rest.

## 8. NASA Countermeasures & Operational Escalation Protocols

### 8.1 Mechanical Fluid-Shift Countermeasures

```
[Level 1: Passive/Local] Swim Goggles (Local IOP Elevation)
       │
       ▼
[Level 2: Venous Sequestration] Veno-occlusive Thigh Cuffs (Braslet-M, 30-50 mmHg)
       │
       ▼
[Level 3: Intrathoracic Decompression] Impedance Threshold Device (ITD Inspiratory Resistance)
       │
       ▼
[Level 4: Volumetric Lower-Body Redistribution] Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP, 25-30 mmHg)
       │
       ▼
[Level 5: Centrifugal Artificial Gravity] Short-Arm Centrifugation (>0.5g vector at eye)
```

#### Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP)
*   **Operational Parameters**: Application of **$25\text{ to }30\text{ mmHg}$ LBNP** encloses the lower extremities below the iliac crest in a sealed vacuum chamber.
*   **Hemodynamic & Ocular Effects**: Sequesters vascular volume in splanchnic and leg venous beds, reducing IJV cross-sectional area, decreasing optic nerve sheath diameter (ONSD), lowering cardiac output, and decreasing ICP by **$1\text{ to }4\text{ mmHg}$ in supine posture** and **$5\text{ to }8\text{ mmHg}$ in $15^\circ\text{ HDT}$ posture.
*   **Operational Constraints**: Higher LBNP ($30\text{ to }50\text{ mmHg}$) increases presyncope risk. Operational deployment targets mobile, low-pressure LBNP suits for multi-hour daily use.

#### Venoconstrictive Thigh Cuffs (Braslet-M)
*   **Operational Parameters**: Custom thigh cuffs tightened to **$30\text{ to }50\text{ mmHg}$** applied to upper thighs during initial spaceflight weeks.
*   **Hemodynamic Effects**: Decreases stroke volume and IJV area, reduces intraocular pressure (IOP), and alleviates facial edema. However, short 2-minute applications at $30\text{ mmHg}$ do not lower central venous pressure (CVP) or ICP.

#### Impedance Threshold Device (ITD)
*   **Operational Parameters**: Inspiratory resistance breathing valve creating negative intrathoracic pressure during inhalation.
*   **Hemodynamic Effects**: Enhances venous return to the heart, consistently lowering CVP by **$3\text{ mmHg}$** and ICP by **$4\text{ mmHg}$**.

#### Swim Goggles (IOP Elevation)
*   **Operational Parameters**: Mild mechanical pressure applied to the periocular area to elevate IOP.
*   **Mechanism**: Increases IOP to offset elevated ICP, reducing the translaminar pressure gradient ($\text{IOP} - \text{ICP}$) across the lamina cribrosa to prevent neuroretinal rim strain.

### 8.2 Nutritional & Pharmaceutical Interventions

*   **B-Vitamin Targeted Supplementation Protocol**: Daily high-dose supplementation with B-complex vitamins (folate, B12, B6) designed to optimize 1-carbon metabolic flux, lower circulating homocysteine, improve endothelial nitric oxide synthase function, and mitigate genetic susceptibility driven by $MTRR$ and $SHMT1$ polymorphisms.
*   **Acetazolamide (Diamox Sequel)**:
    *   *Mechanism*: Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor that suppresses CSF production at the choroid plexus.
    *   *Operational Case Protocol*: Administered to 1 post-flight astronaut presented with Frisén Grade 3 ODE at **$500\text{ mg/day}$ for 6 weeks**, stepped down to **$250\text{ mg/day}$ for 2 weeks** (total 2-month course).
    *   *Therapeutic Outcome & Adverse Effects*: Lumbar puncture opening pressure decreased from $28\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$ to $19\text{ cm H}_2\text{O}$. However, treatment caused a pathological rise in urinary creatinine (up to **$1.7\text{ g/day}$**), fatigue, and paresthesia. Acetazolamide also lowers IOP, which paradoxically worsens the translaminar pressure difference; it is **not approved** as an in-flight SANS countermeasure.
*   **Exenatide (GLP-1 Receptor Agonist)**: Under clinical trial evaluation for reducing CSF secretion at GLP-1 receptors in the choroid plexus to lower ICP without renal toxicity.

## 9. NASA Human Research Roadmap (HRR) Gaps & DAG Integration

### 9.1 Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) Risk Integration
The NASA Human System Risk Board integrates SANS within a Directed Acyclic Graph linking environmental spaceflight hazards to operational failure modes:

*   **Primary Hazard Input**: Altered Gravity ($0\text{g}$) $\rightarrow$ Cephalic Fluid Shift $\rightarrow$ Venous Congestion & ICP Redistribution.
*   **Contributing Hazard Inputs**:
    *   *Ambient $\text{CO}_2$ Risk*: Cerebral arterial vasodilation (managed to ISS 24-hr average $\le 3\text{ mmHg}$).
    *   *Sleep & Circadian Risk*: Impaired glymphatic clearance during sleep cycles.
    *   *Food & Nutrition Risk*: High sodium intake ($3000\text{ to }5000\text{ mg/day}$) exacerbating extracellular fluid expansion and endothelial stiffness.
    *   *Space Radiation Risk*: Heavy ion GCR exposure causing microvascular endothelial damage and blood-retinal barrier breakdown.
*   **In-Mission Consequences**: Structural Ocular Changes $\rightarrow$ Refractive Error Shift $\rightarrow$ Degraded Near Visual Acuity $\rightarrow$ Reduced Individual Crew Readiness $\rightarrow$ Loss of Mission Objectives.
