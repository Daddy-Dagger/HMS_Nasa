## 1. Executive Summary & Integrated Risk Architecture

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Human System Risk Board (HSRB) formally integrated three previously distinct human health risks into a single comprehensive risk title in May 2021: **Risk of Cardiovascular Adaptations Contributing to Adverse Mission Performance and Health Outcomes**.

### 1.1 The Integrated Risk Framework
This integrated risk consolidates the following legacy HSRB risks:
1. **Risk of Orthostatic Intolerance During Re-exposure to Gravity**
2. **Risk of Cardiac Rhythm Problems During Spaceflight**
3. **Risk of Cardiovascular Disease and Other Degenerative Tissue Effects from Radiation Exposure**

### 1.2 Design Reference Mission (DRM) Risk Profiles
* **Low Earth Orbit (LEO) Missions (Short & Long Duration, e.g., ISS)**:
  * Primary In-Flight Concerns: Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) and episodic cardiac dysrhythmias.
  * Primary Post-Flight Concern: Orthostatic Intolerance (OI) during re-entry and landing, resolving within 24 hours to several days post-landing.
  * Radiation Exposure: Low cumulative absorbed dose (30–120 mGy per 6-month stay); baseline cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality rate is comparable to matched terrestrial cohorts.
* **Lunar Surface Missions (Artemis Program)**:
  * In-Flight Acceleration Stress: Orthostatic stress during descent to and ascent from the lunar surface under G-vectors ($+G_z$ head-to-foot accelerations).
  * Fluid Redistribution: Partial gravity ($0.16\text{-G}$) does not significantly mitigate weightlessness-induced headward fluid shifts or internal jugular vein (IJV) congestion.
  * Circadian Environment: The lunar light-dark cycle is 29.5 Earth days, creating circadian desynchronization risks.
* **Mars Planetary Missions (3-Year Duration)**:
  * Autonomous Operations: Communication latency of 15–20 minutes each way prohibits real-time flight surgeon intervention; 100% autonomous crew medical execution required.
  * Partial Gravity Limits: Martian gravity ($0.38\text{-G}$) remains below the threshold ($>0.50\text{-G}$) required to restore normal upright venous hemodynamics.
  * Cumulative Space Radiation: Cumulative galactic cosmic radiation (GCR) and solar particle event (SPE) doses are estimated at 175–450 mGy (approaching or exceeding the 0.5 Gy threshold for elevated lifetime CVD risk).

## 2. In-Flight Cardiovascular Adaptations & Clinical Events

### 2.1 Cardiac Rhythm Disturbances & Dysrhythmias
Spaceflight-induced cardiac rhythm changes have been documented across all NASA flight programs, though weightlessness alone has not been proven as an independent causative factor.

#### Historical In-Flight Dysrhythmia Incidence
* **Mercury & Gemini Programs (1961–1966)**: No clinically significant dysrhythmias reported across single-person or two-person missions up to 14 days or EVAs up to 5.5 hours.
* **Apollo Program (1968–1972)**:
  * *Apollo 15*: One crewmember experienced a 22-beat nodal bigeminal rhythm followed by premature atrial contractions (PACs) during lunar operations, accompanied by severe fatigue. Twenty-one months post-flight, this crewmember suffered a coronary infarction secondary to underlying coronary artery disease.
* **Skylab Station (1973–1974)**: Premature ventricular contractions (PVCs), supraventricular PVCs, two consecutive PVCs during exercise, and episodes of atrioventricular (AV) dissociation preceded by sinus bradycardia were observed during exercise, LBNP, and EVAs.
* **Space Shuttle Program (STS, 1981–2011)**:
  * ECG monitoring during test flights (STS-1 to STS-4) revealed frequent PVCs during re-entry and landing.
  * One-third of Shuttle astronauts flying prior to 1996 experienced PACs or PVCs during EVA.
  * Notable EVA events: One crewmember exhibited 10 minutes of sustained ventricular bigeminy; another experienced frequent PACs.
  * Holter monitor studies (Fritsch-Yelle et al. 1996a; Rossum et al. 1997) showed that baseline hourly rates of PVCs and PACs did not significantly increase in-flight relative to pre-flight baselines.
* **NASA-Mir Program (1994–1998)**:
  * Episodic dysrhythmias recorded in 10 astronauts/cosmonauts at rest, exercise, and EVA.
  * In-flight Holter recording captured an isolated 14-beat non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (NSVT) with a peak heart rate of 215 bpm in one crewmember (who had a similar prior episode).
* **International Space Station (ISS, Expedition 1–Present)**:
  * Khine et al. (2018): 48-hour continuous Holter ECG in 13 astronauts (9 men, 4 women) during 6-month missions showed no statistically significant population-wide increase in PACs/PVCs, and zero cases of atrial fibrillation/flutter in-flight, despite post-flight MRI showing increased left atrial dimensions.

#### Electrophysiological & Etiological Mechanisms
1. **Q-Tc Interval Prolongation**:
   * Changes in Q-Tc reflect spatial and temporal inhomogeneity of ventricular repolarization, predisposing to ventricular dysrhythmias.
   * Compounding Spaceflight Factors: Autonomic nervous system alterations, relative in-flight bradycardia, cardiac chamber remodeling, and onboard QT-prolonging medications (ciprofloxacin, haloperidol, propranolol, verapamil, azithromycin, sertraline, nortriptyline).
2. **Electrolyte Dynamics**:
   * Apollo 15 dysrhythmias were initially attributed to hypokalemia. Serum and urinary potassium dropped by 7% and 47%, respectively.
   * Controlled 12-day bed rest analog studies confirmed potassium deficit alone did not induce clinical dysrhythmias. On landing day of Shuttle/Mir flights, serum potassium, magnesium, and calcium remained within normal clinical reference ranges.
3. **Catecholamine Surge**:
   * Circulating plasma norepinephrine and epinephrine levels in-flight on ISS do not significantly differ from pre-flight seated baselines (Norsk et al. 2015). Peak catecholamine surges occur during launch, EVA, and re-entry.

### 2.2 Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) & Internal Jugular Vein (IJV) Stasis

#### Virchow's Triad in Microgravity
Weightlessness alters every component of Virchow's Triad (stasis, vascular wall change, hypercoagulability):
1. **Venous Stasis**: Elimination of hydrostatic pressure gradients causes a 1–2 liter cephalic fluid shift, expanding central venous volume and markedly distending the internal jugular veins (IJV).
2. **Endothelial Dysfunction**: Prolonged distension and altered shear stress modify vascular lining properties.
3. **Hypercoagulability**: In-flight inflammatory cytokine elevation and hormonal exposures.

#### Empirical Ultrasound Findings on ISS
* **Blood Flow Alterations (Marshall-Goebel et al. 2019)**: Ultrasound evaluation of 11 ISS crewmembers after 50 and 150 days of spaceflight revealed stagnant or retrograde blood flow in the left internal jugular vein in 6 of 11 crewmembers.
* **Incidental Thrombus Findings**: Two astronauts developed left IJV thrombosis during ISS missions:
  * Case 1: Occlusive thrombosis of the left IJV requiring in-flight anticoagulation therapy (Auñón-Chancellor et al. 2020).
  * Case 2: Asymptomatic partial IJV thrombus.

#### Oral Contraceptives & Hypoalbuminemia Link
* **Zwart et al. (2022) Study**: Evaluated female astronauts taking combined estrogen-progestin oral contraceptives (cOC) for menstrual suppression in-flight.
* **Serum Albumin Thresholds**:
  * Terrestrial clinical baseline: Serum albumin $<3.5\text{ g/dL}$ carries a 2.0-fold higher risk of VTE compared to $>4.2\text{ g/dL}$.
  * In-Flight Data: Female crew on cOC exhibited significant hypoalbuminemia. 2 of 6 cOC users had serum albumin $<3.5\text{ g/dL}$ (including the astronaut who developed IJV thrombosis). 2 additional cOC users had levels $<3.8\text{ g/dL}$. Non-cOC females ($N=5$) and male astronauts maintained normal albumin levels.
  * Biomarker Correlations: cOC users demonstrated elevated transferrin and acute-phase inflammatory markers. Third-generation cOC formulations present higher intrinsic VTE risk than second-generation formulations.

## 3. Post-Flight Orthostatic Intolerance (OI) & Vascular Remodeling

### 3.1 Prevalence & Mission Duration Kinetics
Post-spaceflight orthostatic intolerance (inability to maintain upright posture due to hypotension and presyncope) is the primary operational cardiovascular risk during re-entry, landing, and egress.

#### Presyncope Rates on Landing Day (R+0)
* **Short-Duration Flight (Space Shuttle, 5–16 days)**:
  * **20%** presyncope rate during 10-minute $80^\circ$ head-up tilt test (13 of 66 astronauts failed).
* **Long-Duration Flight (Mir & ISS, 4–6 months)**:
  * **$>80\%$** presyncope rate on landing day following Mir missions (5 of 6 astronauts failed $10\text{-min}$ tilt).
  * **66%** presyncope rate following ISS missions (4 of 6 astronauts failed $10\text{-min}$ $80^\circ$ tilt test; Lee et al. 2015).

#### Recovery Kinetics Following Long-Duration Spaceflight
* **Landing Day ($R+0$)**: 66–83% presyncope rate.
* **Return Day 1 ($R+1$)**: Rapid recovery; 8 of 9 ISS astronauts (89%) successfully completed the 10-minute tilt test.
* **Return Day 3 ($R+3$)**: 13 of 15 ISS astronauts (87%) and 19 of 19 Shuttle astronauts (100%) completed the test. Full vascular recovery can take up to several days to weeks.

---

### 3.2 Hemodynamic Mechanisms & Adrenergic Dysfunction
Presyncope during post-flight standing is driven by an inability to compensate for gravity-induced venous pooling in lower extremities.

#### Hemodynamic Responses in Presyncopal vs. Non-Presyncopal Crew
* **Non-Presyncopal Astronauts**: Compensate for reduced stroke volume by mounting a hyper-adrenergic response—marked by significant elevation of total peripheral resistance (TPR) and plasma norepinephrine ($>1000\text{ pg/mL}$ upon standing).
* **Presyncopal Astronauts**: Exhibit adrenergic failure. Despite severe hypovolemia, presyncopal astronauts fail to increase peripheral vascular resistance and display blunted plasma norepinephrine release upon standing ($<400\text{ pg/mL}$ standing vs supine), leading to sudden blood pressure collapse (Meck et al. 2001; Waters et al. 2002).

### 3.3 Plasma Volume Deficits & Fluid Redistribution Kinetics
* **Acute Deficit**: Microgravity exposure induces a rapid plasma volume (PV) loss of **4% to 17%** (average deficit of **$200\text{ to }350\text{ mL}$**).
* **Kinetics**: PV loss occurs within the first 24–48 hours of spaceflight or head-down tilt (HDT) bed rest and reaches a stable plateau.
* **Mechanisms**: Cephalic fluid shift triggers initial central hypervolemia, suppressing the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and vasopressin, driving net fluid shift into interstitial spaces ("third-spacing") and diuresis.
* **Volume vs. Function Disconnect**: Plasma volume loss magnitude is identical between short-duration (14-day) and long-duration (6-month) flights (Meck et al. 2001). The drastically higher OI rate in long missions is driven by structural vascular remodeling, vestibulo-sympathetic reflex blunting, and cardiac atrophy, not hypovolemia alone.

---

### 3.4 Cardiac Remodeling & Left Ventricular Atrophy
Reduced myocardial cardiac work (from reduced stroke volume and diminished gravity loading) leads to progressive cardiac atrophy and chamber remodeling.

#### Quantitative Cardiac Remodeling Metrics
* **Short-Duration Spaceflight / 2-Week Bed Rest**:
  * Left Ventricular Mass (LVM): Decreases by **$5\%\text{ to }8\%$** (Summers et al. 2007; Perhonen et al. 2001a).
  * Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Volume (LVEDV): Decreases by **$7\%\text{ to }10\%$**.
  * Recovery: Full LVM recovery observed within 3 days post-landing for short flights.
* **Long-Duration Spaceflight (4–6 Months) / 60–84 Day Bed Rest**:
  * Left Ventricular Mass (LVM): Decreases by **$8\%\text{ to }15.6\%$** (Platts et al. unpublished; Westby et al. 2016). Reduction is double that of short flights, with persistent atrophy past Day 3 post-landing.
  * Left Ventricular End-Diastolic Volume (LVEDV): Decreases by **$14\%\text{ to }20\%$**.
* **Diastolic Distensibility & Starling Relationship**:
  * Pulmonary Capillary Wedge Pressure (PCWP) and LVEDV relations demonstrate a leftward shift in Starling curves (Levine et al. 1997).
  * Diastolic untwisting and suction are impaired, reducing stroke volume for any given cardiac filling pressure.

### 3.5 Sex Differences in Orthostatic Stress Susceptibility
Female astronauts demonstrate a significantly higher propensity for post-flight orthostatic intolerance compared to male astronauts.

#### Physiological Drivers of Sex Disparity
1. **Vascular Resistance Response**: Female astronauts exhibit lower baseline systemic vascular resistance and blunted vasoconstrictor responsiveness during orthostatic stress (Waters et al. 2002; Fu et al. 2004). Estrogen enhances endothelium-dependent vasodilation.
2. **Splanchnic Capacitance**: Women demonstrate higher splanchnic blood flow pooling and attenuated splanchnic vasoconstriction upon standing.
3. **Myocardial Compliance**: Females possess smaller, stiffer left ventricles with a steeper Frank-Starling slope, causing larger drops in stroke volume for equivalent reductions in cardiac filling pressure.
4. **Bed Rest Tilt Survival Curves (Grenon et al. 2006)**: Following 14–16 days of bed rest, 100% of female test subjects developed presyncope during a progressive tilt protocol ($30^\circ \rightarrow 60^\circ \rightarrow 90^\circ$) in under 32 minutes, whereas over 35% of male subjects completed the full 50-minute stress protocol.

### 4.2 Atrial Fibrillation & Flutter in Astronauts
* **Prevalence**: 36 NASA astronauts diagnosed with atrial fibrillation or flutter through 2019, representing a **10% cumulative prevalence** (36 of 317 cohort; Charvat et al. 2021). Nine were diagnosed during active careers; 9 underwent radiofrequency ablation.
* **Age at Presentation**: Astronauts present with atrial dysrhythmias at a significantly younger age (**43–49 years**) compared to the terrestrial general population (**60+ years**).
* **Etiology**: Attributed to high lifetime aerobic exercise volume (inducing high vagal tone, left atrial enlargement, and prolonged signal-averaged P-wave duration) combined with regular annual clinical screening, rather than in-flight microgravity exposure alone.

### 4.3 Space Radiation & Degenerative Cardiovascular Disease

#### Dose Thresholds & Epidemiological Calibration
* **Linear Dose-Response Above 0.5 Gy**: Terrestrial data from thoracic radiotherapy and Japanese atomic bomb (A-bomb) survivors (Life Span Study) establish a linear dose-dependent increase in major coronary events of **7.4% per Gray** ($95\%\text{ CI: }2.9\text{--}14.5\%/\text{Gy}$; Darby et al. 2013).
* **0.5 Gy-Eq Threshold**: The International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP Publication 118) and NCRP set **0.5 Gy / 0.5 Gy-Eq** as the threshold above which deterministic/degenerative cardiovascular tissue reactions occur.
* **Spaceflight Dosimetry Estimates**:
  * ISS 6-Month Mission: 30 to 120 mGy absorbed dose.
  * Mars 3-Year Exploration Mission: 175 to 450 mGy (up to 0.5+ Gy-Eq). Mars missions approach or exceed the 0.5 Gy threshold for elevated lifetime CVD risk.

#### Advanced Clinical Risk Calculators
* **Astro-CHARM (Astronaut Cardiovascular Health and Risk Modification)**:
  * Baselines the ACC/AHA Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE).
  * Incorporates Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) scoring (Agatston score), high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), family history of premature MI, statin use, and fasting glucose.
  * Significantly improves 10-year CVD risk prediction in fit populations (Khera et al. 2018).
* **3-Year Short-Term Risk Score (Gore et al. 2020)**: Combines PCE with NT-proBNP, high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT), hs-CRP, and CAC/cIMT imaging for short-term mission certification.

### 5.1 Inflight Aerobic & Resistive Exercise Protocols
* **Operational Allocation**: Crewmembers are scheduled **2.5 hours per day**, 6 days per week, on ISS for exercise setup, execution, and hygiene.
* **Hardware Suite**: Advanced Resistive Exercise Device (ARED), T2 Treadmill, and Cycle Ergometer with Vibration Isolation System (CEVIS).
* **Efficacy**:
  * Preserves left ventricular mass and myocardial compliance during 60–70 day bed rest and 6-month ISS flights (Scott et al. 2018).
  * Reduces cardiorespiratory fitness decline (peak $VO_2$ drop attenuated from **-15%** in sedentary controls to **-6%** in NASA Sprint protocol crew; English et al. 2020).
  * *Limitation*: Exercise alone does NOT prevent post-flight orthostatic presyncope on landing day without concurrent fluid or garment countermeasures (Shibata et al. 2010).

---

### 5.2 End-of-Mission Fluid & Salt Loading Formulations

#### NASA Standard Shuttle/ISS Fluid Load Protocol
* **Formulation**: Isotonic solution of salt tablets (Sodium Chloride) and water, or Astro-Ade electrolyte mix.
* **Dosage**: **$0.18\text{ to }0.20\text{ g/kg}$ body weight NaCl** taken with **$18\text{ to }20\text{ mL/kg}$ body weight water** (equivalent to ~1.2 to 1.5 liters of fluid and ~12–14 salt tablets for a 75 kg astronaut).
* **Timing & Retention**: Consumed 2 to 4 hours prior to re-entry deorbit burn. Ground-analog studies (Fortney et al. 1994) demonstrate fluid retention peaks **2.0 to 2.5 hours post-ingestion**, after which renal excretion reduces intravascular volume.

#### Russian Soyuz / Mir Fluid Load Protocol
* **Formulation**: Incremental oral salt/water loading over the final 12–20 hours before landing (18–20 mL/kg solution taken in 3–4 divided doses), supplemented by $0.9\text{ g}$ NaCl in $300\text{ mL}$ water taken during end-of-mission Chibis LBNP sessions.

### 5.4 Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP) & Thigh Cuffs

#### Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP)
* **Physiological Equivalence**: **$-50\text{ mmHg}$** LBNP decompression produces lower-body venous displacement equivalent to standing upright in 1-G.
* **In-Flight Protocol (Russian Chibis)**: Applied during the final 3–4 weeks of ISS missions. Decompression ramp up to **$-45\text{ mmHg}$** in staged intervals combined with leg exercise and oral fluid loading.
* **IJV Decongestion (Marshall-Goebel et al. 2021)**: Low-level LBNP (**$-25\text{ mmHg}$**) while supine reduces internal jugular vein cross-sectional area by **48%** and restores normal anterograde venous flow.

#### Veno-Constrictive Thigh Cuffs ("Braslets")
* **Pressure Specification**: Applied around upper thighs at **$30\text{ to }60\text{ mmHg}$**.
* **Effect**: Reduces IJV cross-sectional area by **$12\%\text{ to }31\%$** and lowers IJV pressure by **$4.8\text{ mmHg}$**, mitigating cephalic fluid congestion.
* *Limitation*: Thigh cuffs do not prevent systemic post-flight orthostatic hypotension when used alone.

---

### 5.5 Artificial Gravity (SRC) & Pharmacotherapy

#### Artificial Gravity (Short Radius Centrifugation)
* **G-Load Targets**: Centrifugation producing **$1.0\text{-G}_z$ at the heart** and **$2.0\text{ to }2.5\text{-G}_z$ at the feet**.
* **Protocols**: Daily 30-to-60 minute sessions (continuous or $5\text{-min}$ intermittent cycles).
* **Efficacy**: Mitigates plasma volume loss and preserves aerobic capacity ($VO_2\text{ peak}$) in 5-to-14 day bed rest studies (Stenger et al. 2012), but 60-day bed rest (AGBRESA study; Kramer et al. 2020) showed AG alone without exercise was insufficient to prevent post-bed rest orthostatic intolerance.

#### Pharmacotherapy & Drug Interactions
* **Midodrine**: Selective $\alpha_1$-adrenergic receptor agonist ($10\text{ mg}$ oral dose administered 1 hour prior to landing). Prevents orthostatic hypotension by increasing peripheral vascular resistance (Platts et al. 2004; Ramsdell et al. 2001).
* **Adverse Drug Interaction (Platts et al. 2006)**: Co-administration of midodrine with **promethazine** (standard anti-emetic used for Space Motion Sickness) induces severe **akathisia** (motor restlessness) due to competitive inhibition of cytochrome P450 isozyme 2D6 (CYP2D6).
* **Fludrocortisone**: Synthetic mineralocorticoid evaluated in EDOMP; proved ineffective and poorly tolerated in-flight.

### Table 6.2 Quantitative Summary of Clinical Countermeasure Protocols

| Countermeasure Category | Protocol & Formulation Parameters | Pressure / Gradient / Dose | Operational Effect & Performance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NASA Fluid Load** | Isotonic NaCl + Water or Astro-Ade solution | $0.18\text{--}0.20\text{ g/kg}$ NaCl + $18\text{--}20\text{ mL/kg}$ water | Consumed 2–4 hr pre-entry; peaks at 2.5 hrs; partially restores PV. |
| **Gradient Garment (GCG)** | 3-Piece elastic stocking + biker short suit | **55 mmHg** (ankle) $\rightarrow$ **35** (knee) $\rightarrow$ **18** (thigh) $\rightarrow$ **16** (abdomen) | **Gold Standard**: Completely eliminates standing SV drop and tachycardia. |
| **Anti-Gravity Suit (AGS)** | Inflatable bladder suit | $75\text{ to }78\text{ mmHg}$ (Full) or $26\text{ mmHg}$ (1-click) | Prevents presyncope; high walking metabolic cost. |
| **Chibis LBNP (Russian)** | Waist-sealed decompression chamber | Staged decompression up to **$-45\text{ mmHg}$** | Performed 3–4 weeks pre-landing; conditions vascular reflexes. |
| **Decongestive LBNP** | Ground/In-flight chamber decompression | **$-25\text{ mmHg}$** low-level vacuum | Reduces IJV cross-sectional area by **48%**; restores anterograde flow. |
| **Thigh Cuffs (Braslets)** | Venoconstrictive leg straps | **$30\text{ to }60\text{ mmHg}$** compression | Reduces IJV area by **12–31%** and IJV pressure by **4.8 mmHg**. |
| **Alpha-1 Agonist (Midodrine)** | Oral vasoconstrictor medication | **$10\text{ mg}$ oral dose** 1 hour pre-landing | Prevents hypotension; **contraindicated with promethazine** (akathisia). |
| **Artificial Gravity (SRC)** | Short Radius Centrifugation | **$1.0\text{-G}_z$ at heart**, **$2.0\text{--}2.5\text{-G}_z$ at feet** | 30–60 min/day maintains $VO_2\text{ peak}$ and PV in short bed rest. |
